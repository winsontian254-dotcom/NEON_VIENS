# DIFFICULTY

## Four ways to be pushed

The last screen of the Suite asks how hard the city should push back. Four buttons answer it. Each of them is a small set of multipliers, applied to everyone who is trying to hurt you, plus a few rules about what you are allowed to do when it goes wrong. None of them changes the story. The same people say the same things and die in the same places; only the cost of getting there is different. You can change the difficulty at any time from the Journal by remaking the character, with one exception that is described below.

## Story

Story is for people who came for the city and the people in it. Enemies deal 45 percent of the normal damage, have 70 percent of the normal health and notice you at 70 percent of the normal rate. Guards stay patient for a long time, drones are fragile and a fight that would end a stranger in a corridor is a stumble. Operations can be restarted or skipped altogether from the pause menu: if a level is giving you trouble, you may carry on with the story without clearing it and no one in the city will mention it. Story is not a lesser way to play. It is the way the writers hoped you might play the first time.

## Operative

Operative is the intended balance, and everything in this handbook is calibrated against it. Enemies deal normal damage, have normal health and notice you at the normal rate. The numbers in the lifepath sections describe the differences between you and an average operator on Operative; on the other difficulties, the same differences hold, but the baseline moves underneath them. If you have no strong feeling about difficulty, choose this one.

## Ghost Protocol

Ghost Protocol is for people who found Operative comfortable. Enemies deal 50 percent more damage, have 30 percent more health and notice you 30 percent faster. This is a smaller change than it sounds and a larger one than it looks: guards who notice you quicker are guards who shoot you sooner, and enemies with more health ask more of your ammunition and your aim. It rewards everything the lifepaths reward. A stealth character is much better off on Ghost Protocol, because each metre of undetected progress is a metre of the harder fight avoided; a heavy fighter will learn to love cover.

## Hardcore

Hardcore is a different game that happens to look the same. Enemies deal twice the normal damage, have 40 percent more health and notice you 50 percent faster. You carry half the ammunition: your reserve limit for every type is halved, and so is what you can pick up, so every round is a decision. The screen and the minimap show no markers. There are no waypoints, no enemy dots and no objective rings; the minimap draws streets and rooms and nothing else, and the only way to find what you need is to look. The Black Market is closed, and so are retries and skips.

And the save is mortal. If you fail an operation on Hardcore, whether by dying or by running out of time, the save is marked dead at that instant and erased when the screen closes. Closing the tab does not help, because the mark is written before anything else is. If you play with an account, the cloud copy is replaced with a marker that cannot be restored. What survives is exactly one thing: the endings you have unlocked stay unlocked, so a Hardcore run that goes badly still counts toward the story. Free roam, story scenes and multiplayer cannot end a Hardcore run; only an operation can.

A Hardcore save also does not go back. Remaking the character in the Journal lets you change every other row, but the difficulty row will refuse anything below Hardcore. That is deliberate. The mode is meant to feel like a promise.

## Choosing

If you are unsure, pick Operative and change it later; you lose nothing by trying. If you are choosing for a lifepath, the sensible matches are these: the Lantern Row Kid is best on Operative or Story, because the lifepath rewards patience and a regeneration that trivialises Story but is a real advantage on Operative; the Helix Contractor is at its sharpest on Ghost Protocol, where early detection matters most; the Glass Wastes Courier is the lifepath for Hardcore, if you are brave, because in a mode with no markers and half the ammunition the ability to keep moving is the only reliable defence.
