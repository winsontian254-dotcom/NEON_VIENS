# PREFACE: WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS

Every terminal in New Avalon asks you who you are, and every terminal in New Avalon is lying about why it wants to know. The Identity Reconstruction Suite is no different, except that this one is honest about one thing: it has no record of you. When the Civic Records Bureau scrubbed the name you were born with, it took your address, your credit history, your childhood and the little blue dot that used to follow you across the city's cameras. What remains is a blank file and a handful of habits you did not know you had.

The Suite exists to fill that file. It does not recover the past; the past is gone, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. What the Suite does is ask you to choose a past that fits the habits your body still remembers, and to decide how hard the city should push back while you find out whether you chose well. These choices are real. They change what you can do in an operation, how guards read your face, how fast your hands reload, how much a stranger's kindness is worth to you, and what the people of New Avalon say when you walk into the room.

This handbook is the only place those consequences are written down. The screens of the Suite are deliberately terse: a name, a picture, a paragraph of mood. They tell you what a lifepath feels like. They do not tell you what it does. That is on purpose. The Bureau's designers believed that nobody reads a manual until something has gone wrong, and that a choice made with a spreadsheet in front of you is a purchase rather than a life. We disagree with the second half of that belief and have written the manual anyway.

You can open this document at any point during creation with the DOSSIER button, or press H, from the Dossier entry on the title screen, and later from the Journal. It is searchable: type a word, a number or the name of a lifepath into the query line (the slash key jumps straight to it) and the record returns every passage that mentions it. Any passage can be extracted into your personal notes with one press of its EXTRACT PASSAGE button, and EXTRACT ALL RESULTS keeps everything a query found. The whole dossier, only your notes, or your own character record from the save on this device can be saved as plain text and carried out of the terminal; in the Android app they open the phone's share sheet, so you can send them to Drive, Notes or mail. The game never sends anything you extract anywhere. The notes live on this device and nowhere else.

A word on tone. The Bureau wrote the first half of this handbook; the second half was added by operators who survived the first. Where they disagree, we have left both opinions in and tried to say which one is correct. Where a number is exact, it is exact. Where a number is rounded, the text says so. If you only read one section, read the one for the lifepath you are about to choose and then the section on attributes. If you read two, add the advice for the first hour at the end. If you read none, you will still be fine, because the game is built to be played without this document. But you asked what the choices mean, and this is where the answer is kept.

# HOW THE RECONSTRUCTION SUITE WORKS

## Four screens, one file

The Suite is a short corridor with four doors. The first door is the lifepath: three cards, one choice. The second is appearance: a column of presets on the left, a preview in the middle and rows of arrows on the right. The third is attributes: five numbers and a handful of points to spend on them. The fourth is difficulty: a window onto the city and four buttons beneath it. Behind every door is a BACK button and a NEXT button. Nothing is saved until you walk through the last door, and nothing you do in the first three can be undone by the fourth, so you may wander back and forth as much as you like.

You can move through the Suite with the mouse, with the arrow keys and Enter, with a controller, or with a finger. ESC steps back one screen, F steps forward and H opens this dossier. On a controller the confirm button selects and the back button retreats. Every row of arrows can be clicked on either side of its value, and the little grid between the arrows opens the full set of colours so you do not have to cycle through them one at a time.

## What gets written

When you press BEGIN the Suite writes a single record: your lifepath, your look, your voice, your five attribute levels and your difficulty. It writes nothing else. It does not decide how the story goes, who lives, who you trust or what you remember; those things are made in play. What the record does decide is a set of numbers that the game reads at the start of every operation: how much Integrity you have, how fast you move, how loud you are, how much a medkit gives back, how long a network gives you before it finds you. The sections that follow list those numbers one at a time.

The record is stored with your save. If you play with an account, it travels with the save to the cloud and back. If you move to another device, the same lifepath and the same attributes come with you. Players in multiplayer sessions wear their chosen look, but the numbers of a lifepath only matter in your own story: in a shared session everyone fights with the same rules so that nobody feels outclassed by a stranger's file.

## Remaking Ghost

The Suite is not a one-time door. At any point in a save you can open the Journal and choose to remake your character. This takes you back through the same four screens with your current choices already filled in. Your progress is kept in full: chapter, story choices, standing with every faction, side quests, weapons, ammunition, upgrades and credits. Only the record changes. Two things are worth knowing. First, changing the lifepath resets your attributes to the new lifepath's minimums, because the minimums belong to the lifepath and not to you. Second, a Hardcore save can be remade in every way except one: it cannot be made easier. If you chose Hardcore, the difficulty row will refuse anything but Hardcore until the save ends.

The old creator, the one with eight backgrounds that you combined two at a time, is gone. Saves made with it were converted without losing anything of value: each old background was folded into the lifepath it most resembled, and each left one point behind in the attribute it was about. A former Street Medic, for example, became a Lantern Row Kid with a point in Technical Ability. If you want to see exactly what changed, remake the character and look at the numbers in the sections below.
